# Cover Letter — Nordic Template

> Replace the [placeholders] below, keep it to about 200 words, and be honest. In the Nordics, a sincere, specific letter beats a polished generic one.

---

Hi [Name / Hiring Team],

I'm applying for the [Position] role at [Company]. I'm a data engineer based in Hangzhou, China, currently open to relocating, and I've been following [Company]'s work in [specific area you genuinely like — e.g. "your data platform blog posts" or "how you handle retail analytics"].

For the past five years I've worked on data warehousing, ETL pipelines, and real-time analytics at companies like Temu and Clobotics. Much of my work has been about the unglamorous but important stuff: making hourly baselines reliable, cutting storage cost, and improving data quality so that teams can trust the numbers. I enjoy the collaborative parts of the job — agreeing on models with business stakeholders, and reviewing each other's SQL with respect.

I'm drawn to [Company] because [specific reason tied to their product, values, or the Nordic way of working — e.g. "your focus on sustainable growth matches how I like to build systems"]. I also value the flat, honest working culture in the Nordics, where good ideas are expected to come from anywhere in the team.

I'd be happy to talk, either in person if you're open to that, or over a video call. You can reach me at ryan.su@noribear.cn or on LinkedIn: [your profile link].

Thanks for reading,

Ryan Su

---

## Why this format works in the Nordics

- **Short and concrete.** ~200 words, no buzzwords, no "highly motivated self-starter".
- **Company-specific.** The letter is where you show you actually looked at the company — this matters more than in many other markets.
- **Team-oriented.** The Nordics value collaboration over individual heroics; a sentence about how you work *with* people goes a long way.
- **No salary, no demands.** Those conversations come later, and both sides know it.
- **Honest and calm.** Lagom — confident but not bragging. If you don't know something, don't pad it.
